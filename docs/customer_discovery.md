
# BranchPulse — Customer Discovery Plan

**Status:** Planned; customer interviews not yet completed.

## 1. Objective

Investigate whether small, multi-property hotel operators have a meaningful problem connecting technology incidents with financial impact and investment decisions.

The objective is to understand existing behaviour and problems, not to persuade people to use BranchPulse.

## 2. Initial Target Segment

**Provisional segment:** Independently operated hotel groups with multiple properties.

**People to interview:**
- Hotel owners or managing directors.
- Operations managers responsible for multiple properties.
- IT managers or outsourced IT providers who influence technology spending.
- Finance managers involved in approving technology investments.

**Initial interview target:** Five exploratory interviews.

These interviews are an initial discovery exercise, not a statistically representative survey.

## 3. Problem Hypotheses

We want to test the following hypotheses rather than assume they are true.

### H1 — Incident visibility

Some operators struggle to compare recurring technology incidents across properties.

### H2 — Financial impact

Some operators cannot easily estimate the financial or operational impact of connectivity-related incidents.

### H3 — Investment decisions

Some operators find it difficult to justify the cost of a proposed technology improvement against the cost of doing nothing.

### H4 — Existing-tool gap

Existing spreadsheets, service providers, IT management tools, or financial systems may not provide the combination of information and workflow these operators need.

### H5 — Willingness to adopt

Some operators may value a lightweight way to compare incidents and investment scenarios.

Interest alone is insufficient evidence of willingness to adopt or pay.

## 4. Interview Introduction

Use this introduction before asking the questions:

"I'm researching how multi-property businesses manage technology disruptions and decide whether improvements are worth the investment. I'm trying to understand your current process and the difficulties you encounter. This is research, not a sales pitch. Please avoid sharing confidential financial, customer, or operational information."

Ask permission before taking notes or recording the discussion.

## 5. Interview Questions

Ask these questions in order. Use follow-up questions when an answer is interesting, but do not lead the interviewee toward a preferred answer.

### A. Understand the business

1. Could you briefly describe your role and the number of properties or locations you oversee?

2. Which technology systems are important to your day-to-day operations?

### B. Understand recent incidents

3. Tell me about the most recent technology disruption that affected a property.

4. What was affected operationally, and how did your team respond?

5. How did you record the incident and determine its impact?

6. Does your organisation compare incidents across properties? Could you walk me through how that works?

### C. Understand investment decisions

7. Think about a recent technology improvement you considered. How did you decide whether it was worth the cost?

8. What information was difficult to obtain or compare when making that decision?

9. Who approves the spending, and what evidence do they expect before approving it?

### D. Understand existing alternatives

10. What tools, spreadsheets, service providers, or internal processes do you currently use?

11. What works well about your current approach?

12. What, if anything, remains difficult or unresolved?

### E. Understand the consequences

13. How frequently does this problem occur?

14. What happens when the impact is not understood or an investment decision is delayed?

15. Have you tried to improve the process before? What happened?

## 6. Optional Prototype Feedback

Ask this section only after understanding the interviewee's existing process.

Show a short demonstration of BranchPulse and explain that its calculations currently use synthetic data and user-entered assumptions.

Ask:

- Which part, if any, relates to a problem you have actually encountered?
- What would be misleading or missing from the current results?
- What information would you need before trusting a report like this?
- How does this compare with what you already use?
- What would make you reject this approach?

Do not treat positive reactions as proof of market demand.

## 8. Interpreting the First Five Interviews

After five conversations, look for repeated patterns.

**Evidence that may strengthen the hypotheses:**
- Interviewees independently describe similar problems.
- They explain concrete consequences of the current process.
- They show that existing workarounds are inadequate.
- They can describe a real investment decision affected by missing information.

**Evidence that may weaken the hypotheses:**
- Their existing processes work adequately.
- Relevant incidents are too infrequent to justify a dedicated tool.
- Financial impact cannot be estimated reliably enough for this workflow.
- Existing products already solve the problem well.
- The person experiencing the problem has no influence over purchasing decisions.

Five interviews cannot establish product-market fit. They can help determine whether further investigation is warranted.

## 9. Next Decision

After completing the interviews:

1. Summarise the strongest recurring problems.
2. Identify contradictions and unanswered questions.
3. Reassess the initial hotel-group segment.
4. Refine the product workflow around demonstrated needs.
5. Decide whether to continue, narrow, or change the problem being investigated.

## 10. Research Integrity

- Never record fictional interviews as real research.
- Do not claim customer validation before conducting interviews.
- Do not collect confidential employer or customer information.
- Keep interviewee identities private unless explicit permission is given.
- Record negative feedback as carefully as positive feedback.
- Clearly distinguish observed evidence from assumptions.
