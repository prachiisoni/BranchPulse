
# BranchPulse — Initial Market Research

**Research date:** 27 October 2026  
**Status:** Initial desk research; customer validation not yet completed.

## 1. Problem hypothesis

Small, multi-location businesses may struggle to connect recurring technology incidents with decisions about which technology improvements deserve investment.

BranchPulse explores whether one lightweight workflow can help them:

- Review estimated incident impact across branches.
- Identify recurring or costly incident types.
- Estimate potentially preventable impact using explicit assumptions.
- Compare investment cost, maintenance, savings, payback, and ROI.
- Export a decision report for discussion with business stakeholders.

This is a hypothesis, not a validated customer problem.

## 2. Initial competitor landscape

### Kaseya — Recovery time and downtime cost calculator

Website: https://www.kaseya.com/resource/rto/

Kaseya provides a calculator for estimating potential technology-outage losses. Its inputs include recovery time, data and backup considerations, employees, salaries, overhead, and business revenue.

**Overlap with BranchPulse:** Estimating the potential cost of technology downtime.

**Question to investigate:** Do users also need a recurring, branch-level view that connects past incidents to proposed investments?

### CPE World — Downtime Cost Calculator

Website: https://cpeworld.org/toolkit/downtime-cost

The calculator considers revenue that is permanently lost, staff productivity, operating hours, outage duration, recovery costs, and penalties.

**Overlap with BranchPulse:** Estimating potential outage costs using explicit business assumptions.

**Question to investigate:** Would businesses find value in tracking incident history over time and comparing investment scenarios, rather than calculating a single outage?

### IBM Apptio — IT Financial Management

Website: https://www.apptio.com/products/ibm-apptio/

IBM Apptio supports broader technology spend management, financial planning, and alignment of IT investments with business outcomes.

**Overlap with BranchPulse:** Connecting technology spending with business value and investment decisions.

**Important distinction:** This is a broader IT financial management product, not a direct equivalent to the current BranchPulse prototype.

**Question to investigate:** Is there a meaningful gap for smaller businesses that need simpler workflows? This cannot be established by product descriptions alone.

## 3. Initial positioning hypothesis

BranchPulse could explore serving small, multi-location operators that experience recurring connectivity or operational technology problems but lack a dedicated IT-finance team.

The initial product hypothesis is:

> Help a business understand where technology incidents are creating the greatest estimated financial exposure, compare potential improvements, and communicate the assumptions behind an investment decision.

Potential initial customer segments to investigate include small hotel groups, restaurant groups, and other businesses that rely on technology across several locations.

No segment has been validated yet. We should choose an initial segment after interviewing potential users.

## 4. Questions for customer discovery

Interview business owners, operations managers, or people responsible for technology spending.

Avoid introducing BranchPulse too early. First, understand how they currently work.

1. Tell me about the last significant technology outage that affected your business.
2. What happened operationally while the problem was occurring?
3. How did you estimate its financial or productivity impact?
4. Who was responsible for deciding what to do about it?
5. Have you considered paying for an improvement to prevent a similar incident? How did you compare the options?
6. What information was missing or difficult to obtain?
7. How do you currently record incidents across different locations?
8. What tools, spreadsheets, or service providers do you already use?
9. How do you justify technology spending to the person who approves the budget?
10. What would make a tool that supports this decision genuinely useful—or unnecessary?

Ask for concrete examples of past behaviour rather than hypothetical promises to use a new product.

## 5. Evidence to collect

For each interview, record:

- Customer segment and number of locations.
- Their current method for recording incidents.
- How they estimate financial impact.
- Their current investment approval process.
- The most difficult part of that process.
- Existing tools and workarounds.
- Whether the problem is frequent and consequential enough to justify change.
- Direct quotes, with permission and without confidential details.

Do not record confidential employer information or sensitive business data without permission.

## 6. What would validate the hypothesis?

Evidence would be stronger if multiple interviewees independently describe a similar problem, demonstrate an existing workaround, and explain the consequences of making poor or delayed investment decisions.

Evidence against the hypothesis would include businesses already solving the problem adequately with existing tools, rarely encountering it, or having no meaningful need to formalise these decisions.

Do not treat compliments, hypothetical interest, or one positive interview as proof of product-market fit.

## 7. Current limitations

- The competitor scan is preliminary, not exhaustive.
- Public product descriptions do not establish comparative usability, pricing, or total cost.
- No customer interviews have been completed for this research.
- BranchPulse currently uses synthetic incident data.
- The proposed differentiation is a hypothesis, not an established competitive advantage.
